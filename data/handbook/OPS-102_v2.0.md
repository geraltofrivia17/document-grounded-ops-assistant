# Change Management Policy

## 1. Purpose

Changes to production systems are the most common cause of avoidable outages. This policy sets out how changes are requested, assessed, approved and recorded so that the risk to depot operations is understood before work starts.

## 2. Types of change

- **Standard change:** a low-risk, pre-approved change that follows a documented procedure, such as adding a printer queue or resetting a scanner profile. Standard changes are logged but do not need Change Advisory Board (CAB) approval.
- **Normal change:** any change that is not standard or emergency. Normal changes need CAB approval.
- **Emergency change:** a change needed urgently to restore a service during a P1 or P2 incident, or to close a serious security vulnerability.

## 3. Submitting a normal change

Raise normal changes in the service portal at least 5 working days before the planned implementation date. The request must include the reason for the change, the systems and sites affected, an implementation plan, a test plan and a back-out plan.

## 4. Change Advisory Board

The CAB meets every Tuesday at 10:00. It is chaired by the Head of IT Operations and includes the system owners and a representative from depot operations. A change that is not reviewed at CAB cannot go ahead, except as an emergency change.

## 5. Emergency changes

An emergency change can be approved by the Duty Operations Manager and the system owner together. Approval can be given by phone but must be recorded in the ticket. Every emergency change must be reviewed retrospectively at CAB within 2 working days of implementation.

## 6. Change freeze

No normal changes are made to warehouse, transport or finance systems from 1 December to 2 January, because this is the peak trading period. Standard and emergency changes are still allowed during the freeze.

## 7. Implementation windows

Normal changes affecting depot systems are implemented between 22:00 and 04:00 unless CAB agrees a different window, because these are the quietest hours for picking and dispatch.
